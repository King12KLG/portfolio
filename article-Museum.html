Wits Cultural Gems

From the Art Museum to the Planetarium and Origins Centre

By Motheo Ngwetjana 19 March 2026 5 minute read

Wits is not just a place of lectures and libraries. Tucked around campus are spaces that hold art, history, and the cosmos itself. Since early 2024 I’ve been visiting these exhibitions and these sites have shaped me.

Wits Art Museum

Threading Through the Collections

30 January – 11 May 2024

The first exhibition I visited as a student. Seeing beadwork, textiles and ceremonial objects pulled from different corners of the continent reminded me how many stories live in a single thread. It felt like an introduction to a much older conversation, and I was finally listening.

Karel Nel: Close and Far

30 May – 7 August 2024

Nel’s layered landscapes blurred the line between the cosmic and the personal. I stood in the gallery feeling small but not insignificant as if the work was asking me to consider my own place within a much larger picture. It was meditative and quietly powerful.

Then I Knew I Was Good at Painting: Esther Mahlangu, A Retrospective

20 November 2024 – 17 April 2025

Walking into this exhibition felt like walking into a celebration. Mahlangu’s bold Ndebele patterns covered every surface with confidence and cultural joy. I left feeling proud, proud of her, proud of the culture she carried, proud of the culture that I am a part of and unexpectedly proud of my own creative path.

Intersections: Bill Ainslie and the Johannesburg Art Foundation

25 November 2025 – 20 March 2026

This show was about teaching and legacy. Seeing work by Ainslie and the artists he nurtured reminded me that creativity rarely happens alone. It made me grateful for the teachers I’ve had, and quietly determined to one day be that person for someone else.

Wits Planetarium

September 2025

In September 2025 I lay back in the dome and watched Our Sky Tonight unfold above me. Stars wheeled across the dark, and for half an hour the city disappeared. I walked out with a quiet sense of wonder with a new appreciation for the vastness we usually ignore.

Origins Centre

The Origins Centre holds rock art replicas, fossil casts, and the deep history of humankind. Walking among the ochre handprints and stone tools, I felt the weight of deep time, a reminder that this campus sits on ancient ground, and that creativity began long before any classroom did.

These spaces are free (or nearly free) and open to students. If you haven't yet visited the Wits Art Museum, the Planetarium, or the Origins Centre just go. There's a cultural campus waiting just behind the lecture halls.
