2 Years Exploring Wits' Natural Surroundings

A guide to the green pockets of campus

By Motheo Ngwetjana 05 April 2026 4 minute read

When you think of Wits, you likely picture lecture halls, the Matrix, and the endless energy of Braamfontein. But during the two years walking this campus I have discovered something quieter. Beneath the concrete buildings lies a network of green sanctuaries, each with its own character. These are my favourite natural pockets, earned through countless afternoons of wandering between classes.

East Campus

The grand old trees of East Campus feel like wise elders. Jacarandas line the pathways, their purple carpet appearing each October like clockwork, while stately plane trees offer deep shade near the Great Hall. On a hot summer day, I gravitate towards the sprawling trees on the library lawn it is wide enough to host a dozen students with laptops and lunch. This side of campus is the universities lung, where the air softens.

West Campus

West Campus has a more tucked-away feel. Here stands a variety of trees and most important the giant bluegum tree, a silent landmark impossible to miss with its pale, peeling trunk and towering height which is a reminder of the campus's history. Nearby, the Tower of Light amid a cluster of palm trees, their leaves catching the Highveld sun in a way that feels unexpectedly coastal, like a small holiday tucked behind the science buildings.

Just beyond West Campus Village, the Wits Barnato Lake sits still and reflective, where I watch the while sitting on the bench ducks drift lazily and a quick lap around its edge can dissolve a stressful morning.

Baranto Hall

My own residence, Baranto Hall, holds a simple courtyard centred around a gathering of trees. Their branches form a dappled roof above the paved area, and I often start my mornings there, looking at the view. It’s a small, rooted stillness right outside my door, a daily reminder that nature on this campus isn’t just landscape — it’s a companion. And when the walk builds an appetite, Olive & Plates offers a shaded table among the campus foliage — a perfect rest stop before the next wander.

Two years have taught me that Wits is not only a university of ideas, but a quiet garden of slow discovery. Skip the shortcut and walk through the green and maybe just maybe you will find your own giant bluegum and your own lake, waiting.
