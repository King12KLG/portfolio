I #HEART JHB

A Guide & Love Letter to the Heart of South Africa

By Motheo Ngwetjana 27 May 2026 5 minute read

Sawubona. Dumela. Goeie dag.

Ahh, Johannesburg the city of gold, where dreams come to rise. This city is truly the heart and soul of this vibrant country, South Africa. Living here all my life, I spent much of it tucked within the familiar confines of home, in a world small, safe, and largely indoors. But everything shifted when I became a student at Wits University, a move that gently took me out of my shell and set me on a path to truly fall in love with the place that raised me.

It started on my very first night at Wits. Standing there as a wide-eyed first-year, no longer under my parents' roof but still only a few kilometres from home, I felt the wave of new freedom. And in that wave, a question bubbled up: "why don't we look around and explore?" From that moment, I became an avid explorer of my University. I'd roam the campus, discovering little pockets I'd never seen, the hidden courtyards, the art-filled museum and quiet corners of the library that smelled of old books and possibility. Those small wanderings transformed the university from a place of study into a treasure map.

A more profound chapter of my exploration came during a first-year FVPA field trip to Sophiatown. Standing in the historic Drum House, I felt the weight of a city's creative soul in the very streets that birthed iconic journalism and jazz. But it was at Christ the King Anglican Church that something truly moved me. The pastor recounted stories of survival during Apartheid, his voice a vessel for memories that still clung to the walls. Right at the entrance, a mural of a mother and her children set against the township's early days caught my gaze and wouldn't let go. I stood there, heart full, understanding that Johannesburg is not just a city of gold but of unbreakable spirit.

Rosebank

By the end of my first semester I knew the university like the back of my hand, and I grew restless. It was time to step outside the gates. The first place I ventured to alone was Rosebank. Taking the Wits bus down the M1, I gazed out at the sprawling human-made forest and it felt like a spectacle stitched just for me. Rosebank became a treat, and not simply because it's a mall. Sure, I'd been going to Sandton City and Hobart Grove my whole life, but Rosebank was special because it was my first solo destination. That independence tasted like freedom.

I'd treat myself to a cone of nougat ice cream at Wedgwood, sink into the Italian-inspired diner glow of Fugazzi, going through new-season threads at Court Order, and sip matcha boba tea at Popo Tea House. At the Rosebank Sunday Market, I bought my very first beaded bracelet — a tiny, colourful token of my newfound self-reliance. That single outing taught me I could go to a mall alone, solve any small problem that came my way, and walk back to the bus stop feeling a little more grown.

44 Stanley

Next, I chased a different kind of thrill at Gold Reef City. I braved the Jozi Express and then the Anaconda, which truly terrified me, but in that stomach-dropping scream I tasted the wild joy of this city's playgrounds. Yet my heart often returns to 44 Stanley, a cultural hub just footsteps from my residence, Baranto Hall. I've lingered over meals at Saint Germain and splurged a whole R44 on heavenly tiramisu at Afters. The Bioscope theatre there recently screened The Devil Wears Prada 2, and I sat in the dark, smiling.

Outdoors & Views

Speaking of hikes if you haven't yet, treat yourself to Walter Sisulu Botanical Gardens. I went recently to celebrate my dear friend Darryn's 22nd birthday, and it was rigorous yet rewardingly beautiful. At the summit we took in a 360-degree view of Johannesburg, from Midrand all the way to the city centre, and felt on top of the world.

For something gentler, there's Zoo Lake, where R20 lets you test your rowing skills and hopefully you fare better than I did. For a different vista, the horse races at Inanda drew me in; on a quiet Saturday afternoon I explored the grounds, watched horses grazing with the Sandton CBD glinting in the distance, and thought, "how many cities can offer this?"

Walkable Neighbourhoods

If you want walkable enchantment, start at Melrose Arch. Stroll through its polished piazza, admire the architecture, pop into art galleries and restaurants that spill onto the cobbles. But for the true walkable heart of student life, nothing beats Braamfontein.

I begin at the Wits Art Museum, then step outside and follow Jorissen Street, cross over Jan Smuts, and turn right onto De Beer Street. There it is — Play Braam, the epitome of Braamfontein's electric student energy. You can shop at BoysofSoweto or S.W.A.N.K, grab a bite at the uniquely designed Playbraam KFC concept store, but the real heartbeat is Kitcheners. That bar has stayed wonderfully unchanged through all the development around it, newly renovated, the time-worn bar hums with a spirit that is irrefutably Joburg.

Johannesburg is truly a place for all, with so much to do and explore that a single article can never fully capture its magnificent vibrancy. I still have a whole list of places to uncover, and right at the top sits Hugh's a new jazz bar named after the legend himself, Hugh Masekela. Because that's what this city is: a place of celebration, rhythm, and endless discovery. I'll see you there and on every street corner in between.
